Data Interpretation, module one: reading a table. This is a study lesson. All numbers are made up. As on 26 November 2026, the SSC CGL 2026 notice has quantitative aptitude in Tier one, and the IBPS PO notification has a Data Analysis and Interpretation test in Main. This lesson is about reading tables. It does not say what any paper will ask.

First, read before you calculate. Check the title, the unit, and what each row and column means. A common cause of mistakes is the wrong row or the wrong unit.

Here are five useful calculations for these table drills. Total: add the cells asked about. Average: total divided by the count of values you added. Percentage change: new minus old, divided by old, times one hundred. The base is always the old value. Share: part divided by total, times one hundred. Ratio: write both values and divide by the common factor.

Example. A shop sold 120 in year one and 180 in year three. The change is 60, the base is 120, so the rise is 50 percent. A fall from 180 back to 120 is 60 over 180, which is 33 point 3 percent, not 50. The base changed.

For speed, round decimals only at the end, as much as needed, compare answer options before long division, and do not add a whole table when two cells are enough. Now try the eight practice questions and recheck each one.
