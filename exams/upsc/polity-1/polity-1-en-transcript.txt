Polity, module one. The Preamble and key Fundamental Rights. This is a short study lesson. This is an explanation based on the official Constitution of India text, read on 25 November 2026. It paraphrases and summarizes the articles. It is not a word-for-word quotation, and the exact wording is in the text itself.

Part one. The Preamble.

It begins: We, the people of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic. It then secures to all citizens justice, social, economic and political. Liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship. Equality of status and of opportunity. And it promotes fraternity, assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation. It says it was adopted in the Constituent Assembly on the twenty-sixth day of November, 1949.

A memory aid. Four goals: justice, liberty, equality, fraternity. Five words describing India: sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic, republic. The words Socialist and Secular, and the word integrity, were added by the Forty-second Amendment Act of 1976, in force from 3 January 1977.

Part two. The articles.

Article 14. The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India. Note the words any person. Not only citizens.

Article 15, clause 1. The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth or any of them. Note two words: citizen, and only. Clause 2 covers access to shops, restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment, and the use of wells, tanks, roads and public places. Clause 3 allows special provision for women and children. Clauses 4, 5 and 6 allow special provision for backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Tribes, and economically weaker sections. Clause 6 came from the 103rd Amendment of 2019.

Article 19, clause 1. Citizens have the right to freedom of speech and expression, to assemble peaceably without arms, to form associations, unions or cooperative societies, to move freely throughout the territory of India, to reside and settle in any part of the territory of India, and to practise any profession, occupation, trade or business. Sub-clause f, the right to acquire, hold and dispose of property, was omitted by the Forty-fourth Amendment Act of 1978. Clause 2 allows reasonable restrictions on speech for sovereignty and integrity, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, and incitement to an offence.

Article 20 has three protections. One, no conviction except under a law in force at the time of the act, and no heavier penalty than the law then allowed. Two, no one is prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once. Three, no accused person is compelled to be a witness against himself.

Article 21. No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.

Article 21A. Free and compulsory education for children aged six to fourteen, in the manner the State decides by law. It was inserted by the Eighty-sixth Amendment Act of 2002, in force from 1 April 2010. It was not in the 1950 text.

Article 32. The right to move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights is guaranteed. The Court can issue directions, orders or writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari.

Quick rule. Any person: Articles 14, 20 and 21. Citizens only: Article 15, clauses 1 and 2, and Article 19.
