English grammar basics: subject-verb agreement and a versus an. This is original study material, not a previous-year paper. The SSC CGL 2026 Notice, paragraph 13.10.4, says the English test checks the ability to understand correct English, basic comprehension and writing ability. It does not list grammar topics, so these topics are our own mapping to correct English. The rules come from the Purdue OWL writing guide, an American university guide, not an exam board.

The verb must match the number of its subject.

One. Distinct subjects joined by and usually take a plural verb. This is not a universal rule. Ravi and his cousins are at the fair.
Two. Singular nouns joined by or take a singular verb. The pen or the pencil is on the table.
Three. With a singular and a plural joined by or, the verb agrees with the nearer one. The boy or his friends go to school every day. His friends or the boy goes to school every day.
Four. Doesn't goes with a third-person singular subject, like he, she or it. Don't goes with plural subjects, and also with I and you.
Five. A phrase between the subject and the verb does not change the verb. One of the boxes is open.
Six. Each, everyone, someone, nobody are singular. Each of the students is present today.
Seven. News and mathematics take singular verbs. The news is on at six.
Eight. Scissors and trousers take plural verbs. These scissors are sharp.
Nine. In simple there is or there are sentences, the verb agrees with the noun that follows. There are many questions.
Ten. Collective nouns like team and family take a singular verb in this American guide. In British English, Oxford says they can take a singular or a plural verb. So this is not one universal rule, and this lesson has no practice question on it.
Also, with, together with, including and as well as do not change the number of the subject.

Now a versus an. The choice depends on sound, not spelling. A vowel sound takes an. A consonant sound takes a. So, an honest mistake, because the h is silent. But a union and a unicorn, because they begin with a you sound.
