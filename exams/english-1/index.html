English: Subject-Verb Agreement और "a" बनाम "an" (exam notes)

Module eng1। Scope: Subject-Verb Agreement के ग्यारह नियम और "a/an" की ध्वनि का नियम। Tenses, prepositions, vocabulary, comprehension passages और writing इसमें नहीं हैं। छोटे grammar नोट्स और मौलिक अभ्यास, previous-year questions नहीं। कोई trend या weightage claim नहीं। उदाहरण वाक्य English में ही रहते हैं।

1. Official syllabus में स्थान

SSC CGL 2026 Notice, para 13.10.4 (English Comprehension, Tier-I) कहता है कि परीक्षा में उम्मीदवार की "ability to understand correct English, his basic comprehension and writing ability, etc." जाँची जाएगी। इसमें grammar के topics सूचीबद्ध नहीं हैं। इसलिए Subject-Verb Agreement और articles हमारा "correct English" से जोड़ा गया mapping है, SSC का नामित topic नहीं। Notice 2026 का दस्तावेज़ है, इसलिए इसके pattern दूसरे वर्षों के लिए पुराने हैं। दूसरी परीक्षाओं के अपने syllabus हैं।

2. Subject-verb agreement (Purdue OWL "Subject/Verb Agreement")

Verb की संख्या (singular या plural) subject के अनुसार होनी चाहिए।

  1. "and" से जुड़े दो या अधिक अलग-अलग subjects: आम तौर पर plural verb। Ravi and his cousins are at the fair. कुछ अपवाद होते हैं जहाँ "and" एक ही चीज़ के हिस्सों को जोड़ता है; वे यहाँ शामिल नहीं हैं, इसलिए यह सार्वभौमिक नियम नहीं है।
  2. "or" या "nor" से जुड़े singular nouns: singular verb। The pen or the pencil is on the table.
  3. "or" या "nor" से जुड़े एक singular और एक plural: verb उस noun के अनुसार जो verb के पास है। The boy or his friends go to school every day. His friends or the boy goes to school every day.
  4. "Doesn't" third-person singular subject (he, she, it, the boy) के साथ। "Don't" plural subjects के साथ, और साफ़ तौर पर "I" और "you" के साथ भी। He doesn't like it. They don't like it.
  5. Subject और verb के बीच का phrase verb नहीं बदलता। One of the boxes is open. The team captain, as well as his players, is anxious.
  6. Each, each one, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anybody, anyone, nobody, somebody, someone, no one singular हैं। Each of the students is present today.
  7. civics, mathematics, measles, news जैसे nouns के साथ singular verb। The news is on at six। पैसे की राशि: "Five dollars is a lot of money" (singular), पर जब dollars नाम की मुद्रा की ही बात हो: "dollars are often used instead of rubles"।
  8. Scissors, tweezers, trousers, shears के साथ plural verb। These scissors are sharp.
  9. सरल "there is" या "there are" वाक्यों में verb उस noun के अनुसार आता है जो बाद में आता है। There are many questions. There is a question. यह नोट सिर्फ़ ऐसे सरल उदाहरणों के लिए है, हर "there" वाक्य-रचना के लिए नहीं।
  10. Collective nouns (group, team, committee, class, family): Purdue OWL के अनुसार singular verb। The team runs during practice. ब्रिटिश प्रयोग अलग है: Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary के अनुसार British English में collective noun के साथ singular या plural verb आ सकता है, जबकि American English में singular ही आता है। Cambridge Dictionary की British grammar के अनुसार जब समूह एक इकाई माना जाए तो singular, और जब अलग-अलग सदस्य माने जाएँ तो plural (The audience was larger than average / The audience were all cheering wildly)। इसलिए यह एक सार्वभौमिक नियम नहीं है, और इस lesson में collective noun का कोई अभ्यास प्रश्न नहीं है। साथ ही (OWL पेज का नियम 11): "with", "together with", "including", "accompanied by", "in addition to", "as well as" संख्या नहीं बदलते। The President, accompanied by his wife, is travelling to India.

3. "a" बनाम "an" (Purdue OWL "Articles: A versus An")

  • चुनाव पहले अक्षर की ध्वनि पर है, वर्तनी पर नहीं। Vowel ध्वनि: an। Consonant ध्वनि: a।
  • Silent "h": an honest error, an honorable peace।
  • जब "u" का उच्चारण "you" जैसा और "o" का "w" जैसा हो: a union, a unicorn, a one-legged man।
  • उदाहरण: a cat, an apricot, an Indian, a U.S. ship।

4. कहां सावधानी रखें

  • Purdue OWL एक अमेरिकी विश्वविद्यालय की writing guide है, परीक्षा बोर्ड नहीं। इसका collective-noun नियम अमेरिकी नियम है; ब्रिटिश प्रयोग में singular या plural दोनों verb आ सकते हैं। अमेरिकी पसंद को ही एकमात्र सही रूप न मानें। अगर प्रश्न सिर्फ इसी बिंदु पर टिका हो, तो देखें कि समूह एक इकाई की तरह काम कर रहा है या अलग-अलग सदस्यों की तरह।
  • "Don't" I, you और plural subjects के साथ आता है। he, she, it और दूसरे third-person singular subjects के साथ "doesn't" लगाएँ।
  • नियम 5 में phrase के अंदर वाले पास के noun पर नियम न लगाएँ, असली subject पर लगाएँ।

5. Quick recall

अलग-अलग subjects "and" से जुड़े = आम तौर पर plural। or/nor = पास वाला noun। Each, everyone, someone = singular। News, mathematics = singular। Scissors, trousers = plural। सरल "there is/are" वाक्यों में "there" subject नहीं है। a/an ध्वनि से: an honest, a union।

English: Subject-Verb Agreement and "a" versus "an" (exam notes)

Module eng1. Scope: eleven subject-verb agreement rules and the "a/an" sound rule. Not tenses, prepositions, vocabulary, comprehension passages or writing. Short grammar notes with original practice, not previous-year questions. No trend or weightage claims.

1. Where this sits in the official syllabus

The SSC CGL 2026 Notice, para 13.10.4 (English Comprehension, Tier-I), says the test checks the candidate's "ability to understand correct English, his basic comprehension and writing ability, etc." It does not list grammar topics. So subject-verb agreement and articles are our mapping to "correct English", not a topic named by SSC. The notice is a 2026 document, so patterns in it are historical for other years. Other exams have their own syllabi.

2. Subject-verb agreement (Purdue OWL "Subject/Verb Agreement")

The verb must match its subject in number.

  1. Two or more distinct subjects joined by "and": usually a plural verb. Ravi and his cousins are at the fair. Exceptions exist where "and" joins parts of one single thing; they are not covered here, so this is not a universal rule.
  2. Singular nouns joined by "or" or "nor": singular verb. The pen or the pencil is on the table.
  3. A singular and a plural joined by "or" or "nor": the verb agrees with the nearer one. The boy or his friends go to school every day. His friends or the boy goes to school every day.
  4. "Doesn't" goes with a third-person singular subject (he, she, it, the boy). "Don't" goes with plural subjects and, explicitly, with "I" and "you". He doesn't like it. They don't like it.
  5. A phrase between subject and verb does not change the verb. One of the boxes is open. The team captain, as well as his players, is anxious.
  6. Each, each one, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anybody, anyone, nobody, somebody, someone, no one are singular. Each of the students is present today.
  7. Nouns like civics, mathematics, measles and news take singular verbs. The news is on at six. Money amounts: "Five dollars is a lot of money" (singular), but "dollars are often used instead of rubles" when meaning the dollars themselves.
  8. Scissors, tweezers, trousers, shears take plural verbs. These scissors are sharp.
  9. In simple "there is" or "there are" sentences, the verb agrees with the noun that follows. There are many questions. There is a question. This note covers only such simple examples, not every "there" construction.
  10. Collective nouns (group, team, committee, class, family): the Purdue OWL guidance takes a singular verb. The team runs during practice. British usage differs: Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary says a collective noun can take a singular or a plural verb in British English and must take a singular verb in American English. Cambridge Dictionary's British grammar says the verb can be singular when the group is seen as one unit and plural when seen as individuals (The audience was larger than average / The audience were all cheering wildly). So this is not a single universal rule, and this lesson has no collective-noun practice question. Also (rule 11 on the OWL page): "with", "together with", "including", "accompanied by", "in addition to", "as well as" do not change the number. The President, accompanied by his wife, is travelling to India.

3. "a" versus "an" (Purdue OWL "Articles: A versus An")

  • The choice depends on the sound of the first letter, not the spelling. Vowel sound: an. Consonant sound: a.
  • Silent "h": an honest error, an honorable peace.
  • "u" sounding like "you" and "o" sounding like "w": a union, a unicorn, a one-legged man.
  • Examples: a cat, an apricot, an Indian, a U.S. ship.

4. Where to be careful

  • Purdue OWL is an American university writing guide, not an exam board. Its collective-noun rule is the American one; British usage allows a singular or plural verb. Do not treat the American preference as the only correct form. If a question turns only on this point, read the sentence for whether the group acts as one unit or as individuals.
  • "Don't" goes with "I", "you" and plural subjects. Use "doesn't" for he, she, it and other third-person singular subjects.
  • Do not apply a rule to a nearer noun inside a phrase (rule 5), only to the true subject.

5. Quick recall

distinct subjects joined by and = usually plural. or/nor = nearer noun. Each, everyone, someone = singular. News, mathematics = singular. Scissors, trousers = plural. In simple "there is/are" sentences, "there" is not the subject. a/an follow sound: an honest, a union.

10 original MCQs · answers included

eng1 practice (original practice questions, NOT SSC or any exam's questions; rules from sources.md)

  1. Ravi and his cousins ___ at the fair. (a) is (b) are (c) am (d) has been Answer: (b). Two distinct subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb here.

  2. The pen or the pencil ___ on the table. (a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been Answer: (c). Two singular nouns joined by "or" take a singular verb.

  3. The boy or his friends ___ to school every day. (a) go (b) goes (c) is going (d) has gone Answer: (a). The verb agrees with the nearer noun, "friends".

  4. His friends or the boy ___ to school every day. (a) go (b) goes (c) are going (d) have gone Answer: (b). The nearer noun is "boy".

  5. He ___ like it. (a) don't (b) doesn't (c) do not (d) aren't Answer: (b).

  6. One of the boxes ___ open. (a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been Answer: (b). The subject is "one", not "boxes".

  7. Each of the students ___ present today. (a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been Answer: (c). "Each" is singular.

  8. The news ___ on at six. (a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been Answer: (b).

  9. These scissors ___ sharp. (a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been Answer: (b).

  10. Choose the correct indefinite article: It was ___ honest mistake. (a) a (b) an (c) the (d) both a and an Answer: (b). The "h" is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound.
