RPSC RAS Prelims, State Political System of Rajasthan. This is a study lesson, not a previous-year paper, and it says nothing about other states. It uses the 2024 syllabus pattern, not a current notification.

Four items: the Governor, the Chief Minister with the Council of Ministers, the Legislative Assembly, and the High Court.

The Governor first. Article 153 says each State has a Governor. Article 154: the executive power of the State is vested in the Governor. Article 155: the President appoints the Governor by warrant under his hand and seal. Article 156: the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President, and may resign by writing to the President. Subject to that, the term is five years. When the five years end, he stays on until his successor takes office. That applies to a term that has expired. It does not keep in office a Governor who has resigned or whose tenure at the President's pleasure has ended.

Now the Council of Ministers. Article 163: a Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head aids and advises the Governor, except where the Constitution requires the Governor to act in his discretion. Article 164: the Governor appoints the Chief Minister. The other Ministers are appointed by the Governor on the Chief Minister's advice. The total number of Ministers, including the Chief Minister, cannot be more than fifteen percent of the Assembly's members, and not fewer than twelve. That came with the Ninety-first Amendment in 2003. As my own calculation, fifteen percent of two hundred is thirty. And the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

The Legislative Assembly. Rajasthan's Assembly has two hundred seats, according to the Assembly's own Introduction page, which also says Rajasthan chose a legislature of one House. Article 170 gives the general rule, subject to Article 333: a State Assembly has at most five hundred and at least sixty members, directly elected. A few States have smaller minimums under special provisions, but Rajasthan, with two hundred seats, falls under the general rule. Article 172: it continues five years from its first meeting, unless dissolved earlier. While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, Parliament can extend it by law, one year at a time, but never beyond six months after the Proclamation has ceased to operate.

The High Court. Article 214: a High Court for each State. Article 217: a Judge is appointed by the President by warrant, and, unless additional or acting, holds office until age sixty-two. How names are recommended is outside this lesson. To qualify, a person must be a citizen of India with at least ten years in a judicial office, or at least ten years as a High Court advocate. According to the High Court's own history page, Rajasthan's High Court has its principal seat at Jodhpur from the first of November 1956, and a permanent bench at Jaipur. The Jaipur bench was abolished in 1958 and re-established in January 1977, according to the High Court's annual reports.

The article text comes from the official edition of the Constitution that includes the hundred-and-sixth Amendment of 2023, and the lesson uses only clauses in operation in that edition. As on the twenty-fifth of November 2026, the official list of Constitution amendment Acts ends with the hundred-and-sixth.
