Geography of India, southwest monsoon mechanism and rainfall categories. This is a short study note from India Meteorological Department material. It is not official, and it is not a previous year paper. It covers the mechanism, not regional rainfall maps.

In northern summer, land around the Arabian Sea heats up and a shallow low pressure trough, the heat low, forms over northwest India and Pakistan. The monsoon trough runs from that heat low to the head of the Bay of Bengal. When the trough shifts south, the monsoon is strong and active over India. When it shifts north, toward the Himalayan foothills, we get a break, with heavy rain on the foothills.

Other features to remember: the Tibetan High, the Tropical Easterly Jet, the Somali jet, which reaches India's west coast in June and is strongest in July, and the Mascarene High in the south Indian Ocean.

The monsoon normally sets in over Kerala around first June. I M D's revised normal for covering the whole country is around eighth July. Older material may say fifteenth July, which is the older normal. Withdrawal criteria for extreme northwest India are considered only after first September.

Now rainfall categories. Check the scale first. For daily to monthly rainfall over districts and states, I M D calls a departure of minus nineteen to plus nineteen percent from the long period average normal. For June to September rainfall over the whole country, I M D's current five categories, as in its April 2026 forecast release, call ninety-six to one hundred four percent of the long period average normal. The current base is eighty-seven centimetres, for 1971 to 2020. An older I M D FAQ gives different numbers, so we do not teach them.

Break spells are dry intervals in July and August. Active spells are wetter than normal. Dates here are normals, not dates for every year. Now try the ten practice questions.
