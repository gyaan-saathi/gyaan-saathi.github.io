Geography of Rajasthan, physiographic regions and climate. This is exam study material, not a previous-year paper. It covers only the first two points of the Geography of Rajasthan section in the RPSC Prelims 2024 syllabus. It is not a current notification.

First, the state. Rajasthan covers three hundred forty-two thousand, two hundred thirty-nine square kilometres, about ten point four percent of India, the largest state by area, according to the Economic Review 2024 to 25, the Forest Survey of India and the Environment Department page. The highest peak is Guru Shikhar on Mount Abu, one thousand seven hundred twenty-two metres, according to the Environment Department page.

The sources used here name four physiographic regions.

One, the Western Desert or Western Plain. Barren hills, level rocky plains and sandy plains. The Thar Desert lies northwest of the Aravalli.

Two, the Aravalli Range. In Rajasthan it runs from Guru Shikhar in the southwest to Khetri in the northeast. A Rajasthan government report says the range runs from Gujarat to Delhi, so it continues outside Rajasthan. The Environment Department page says it divides the state into about sixty percent to the northwest and forty percent to the southeast.

Three, the Eastern Plains, northeast, east and southeast of the Aravalli, with rich alluvial soils.

Four, the South-Eastern Plateau, which the Rajasthan Foundation page also calls the Hadoti Plateau.

The contrast to remember: northwest of the Aravalli is sandy with little water, improving toward the east. In general, the southeast of the Aravalli is higher, more fertile and more varied. The Environment Department page describes that wide area with a tableland around Kota and Bundi and badlands along the Chambal River. That is not a description of the Hadoti Plateau alone.

Climate. The Rajasthan Foundation page says the climate ranges from arid to humid, with four seasons, named pre-monsoon, which is the hot season with hot winds and dust storms, then monsoon, post-monsoon and winter. This lesson does not teach exact month boundaries. The Forest Survey of India says the state's climate varies from semi-arid to arid, with the west, including the Thar Desert, relatively dry. The sources use different labels, so learn the pattern: the west is drier than the east and south, summers are hot, and there are four seasons.

A note on limits. Page figures for the share of each of the four regions and for the Aravalli's length differ, and some pages are undated, so this lesson does not teach those, or rainfall and temperature numbers. The sixty-forty split is different. It divides the state into the part northwest of the Aravalli and the part southeast of it, and it is not the share of any one region.
