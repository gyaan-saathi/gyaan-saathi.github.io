RPSC RAS Prelims, Rajasthan history. Topic: the integration of Rajasthan. This is a study lesson, not a previous-year paper, and it says nothing about other states.

First, where it sits in the syllabus. The RPSC Prelims 2024 syllabus PDF has one paper, General Knowledge and General Science, with 150 questions of equal marks, 200 marks in all, and three hours. The PDF also mentions negative marking, but it does not say how many marks each question carries, so we will not do any marking sums here. Check the notification for the details. That is the 2024 pattern, and other years may differ. The history section of that syllabus ends its modern Rajasthan line with the integration of Rajasthan. That is today's topic. The same line also lists Praja Mandal and other movements, which this lesson does not cover.

At Independence, Rajputana had nineteen princely states and three chiefships. They joined in stages, from March 1948 to the first of November 1956. The Rajasthan Legislative Assembly table and the Rajasthan Foundation page list seven stages.

Stage one. Matsya Union, seventeenth March 1948. Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur and Karauli.

Stage two. Rajasthan Union, twenty-fifth March 1948, with Kota as capital. Banswara, Bundi, Dungarpur, Jhalawar, Kishangarh, Kota, Pratapgarh, Shahpura and Tonk.

Stage three. Udaipur joined, eighteenth April 1948. Pandit Nehru inaugurated it, according to the Assembly page.

Stage four. Greater Rajasthan, thirtieth March 1949. Bikaner, Jaipur, Jaisalmer and Jodhpur joined.

Stage five. The Matsya Union merged, fifteenth May 1949.

Stage six. Twenty-sixth January 1950. The eighteen states and the princely state of Sirohi came together, with Abu and Delwara left out, according to the Assembly table.

Stage seven. First November 1956, under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. Ajmer, Abu Road taluka and the Sunel Tappa region joined Rajasthan. Abu Road came from Bombay State, and Sunel Tappa from Madhya Bharat. In the other direction, the Sironj sub-division of Kota district went to Madhya Pradesh. The Assembly table calls Sironj a part of Jhalawar district, but the Act says Kota district, so we follow the Act.

A caution. Official pages count the phases differently, five, six or seven, so go by the dates and read what the question asks. Some prep sites give eighteenth March for stage one, but the two government sources used here say seventeenth March.
